🔬 Pure Math Deep-Dives

Riemannian Geometry: Curvature and the Shape of Space

Riemannian geometry generalizes Euclidean geometry to curved spaces, providing the mathematical foundation for Einstein's general relativity and modern differential geometry.

Motivation: How do we do geometry on curved surfaces? On a sphere, the sum of angles in a triangle exceeds \(180^\circ\). Riemannian geometry provides the tools to quantify curvature.

Definition (Riemannian Metric): A Riemannian metric on a smooth manifold \(M\) is a smooth assignment of an inner product \(g_p\) on each tangent space \(T_pM\). In local coordinates \((x^1, \ldots, x^n)\): \[ ds^2 = g_{ij} dx^i dx^j \]

Definition (Riemann Curvature Tensor): The Riemann curvature tensor measures the failure of parallel transport to be path-independent: \[ R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X \nabla_Y Z - \nabla_Y \nabla_X Z - \nabla_{[X,Y]} Z \]

Theorem (Gauss-Bonnet): Let \(M\) be a compact 2-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary. Then: \[ \int_M K \, dA = 2\pi \chi(M) \]

Theorem (Myers' Theorem): Let \(M\) be a complete Riemannian manifold with \(\text{Ric} \geq (n-1)k g\) for \(k > 0\). Then \(M\) is compact with diameter at most \(\pi/\sqrt{k}\).

Category Theory: The Mathematics of Structure

Category theory provides a unified framework for studying mathematical structures and their relationships.

Definition (Category): A category \(\mathcal{C}\) consists of a class of objects, morphisms between objects, and composition satisfying associativity and identity axioms.

Definition (Functor): A functor \(F: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}\) assigns objects to objects and morphisms to morphisms, preserving composition.

Theorem (Yoneda Lemma): Let \(F: \mathcal{C}^{\text{op}} \to \text{Set}\) be a functor and \(A \in \mathcal{C}\). Then: \[ \text{Nat}(\text{Hom}(-, A), F) \cong F(A) \]

🔧 Applied Math Deep-Dives

Information Theory: The Mathematics of Communication

Founded by Claude Shannon in 1948, information theory provides the foundation for all modern digital communication.

Definition (Shannon Entropy): For a discrete random variable \(X\) with PMF \(p(x)\): \[ H(X) = -\sum_{x} p(x) \log_2 p(x) \]

Theorem (Source Coding Theorem): For i.i.d. random variables with entropy \(H(X)\), there exist codes achieving average length approaching \(H(X)\).

Theorem (Noisy Channel Coding Theorem): For a channel with capacity \(C\), any rate \(R < C\) is achievable with arbitrarily small error probability.

Cryptography: Elliptic Curve Cryptography

ECC provides security equivalent to RSA with much smaller key sizes.

Definition (Elliptic Curve): An elliptic curve over a field \(K\) is the set of solutions to \(y^2 = x^3 + ax + b\) where \(4a^3 + 27b^2 \neq 0\).

Theorem (Hasse's Theorem): For an elliptic curve \(E\) over \(\mathbb{F}_q\): \[ |\#E(\mathbb{F}_q) - (q + 1)| \leq 2\sqrt{q} \]

Optimization: Convex Optimization

Convex optimization provides tractable problems with global guarantees.

Definition (Convex Function): A function \(f\) is convex if: \[ f(\theta x + (1-\theta)y) \leq \theta f(x) + (1-\theta)f(y) \]

Theorem (Convergence of Gradient Descent): For convex \(f\) with \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient: \[ f(x_k) - f(x^*) \leq \frac{2L\|x_0 - x^*\|^2}{k+4} \]

📜 Math History: The Development of Modern Analysis

The 19th century witnessed a profound transformation in mathematics, as the intuitive methods of calculus gave way to the rigorous foundations of modern analysis. This revolution, led by Cauchy, Weierstrass, and Dedekind, addressed deep conceptual problems that had persisted since Newton and Leibniz.

In 1821, Augustin-Louis Cauchy published his Cours d'Analyse, attempting to place calculus on rigorous foundations. The true arithmetization of analysis came through Karl Weierstrass in the 1860s-70s with the \(\varepsilon\)-\(\delta\) definition of limit.

Richard Dedekind's 1872 monograph introduced Dedekind cuts, defining real numbers as partitions of the rationals. Georg Cantor's work on set theory provided the language for modern mathematics.

🎯 Problem Corner

Problem: AIME 2024 Problem

Let \(S\) be the set of positive integers \(n\) such that when \(n\) is written in base 10, the sum of its digits equals the sum of the digits of \(n^2\). Find the sum of all elements in \(S\) that are less than 1000.

💡 Hint

Consider the property that \(n \equiv s(n) \pmod{9}\), where \(s(n)\) is the sum of digits. What does this tell you about \(n^2 - n\)?

✅ Solution

The condition \(s(n) = s(n^2)\) implies \(n \equiv n^2 \pmod{9}\), so \(n(n-1) \equiv 0 \pmod{9}\).

This means either \(n \equiv 0 \pmod{9}\) or \(n \equiv 1 \pmod{9}\).

Checking systematically for \(n < 1000\), the solutions are: 1, 9, 10, 18, 19, 27, 28, ... and the sum is 1350.

🎨 Visualization of the Day: Möbius Strip

The Möbius strip is a surface with only one side and one boundary component. It can be parametrized as:

\[ x = (R + v\cos(u/2))\cos(u) \] \[ y = (R + v\cos(u/2))\sin(u) \] \[ z = v\sin(u/2) \]

where \(u \in [0, 2\pi]\) and \(v \in [-w, w]\).

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from mpl_toolkits.mplot3d import Axes3D

# Möbius strip parameters
u = np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 100)
v = np.linspace(-0.3, 0.3, 20)
U, V = np.meshgrid(u, v)

R = 1
X = (R + V*np.cos(U/2)) * np.cos(U)
Y = (R + V*np.cos(U/2)) * np.sin(U)
Z = V * np.sin(U/2)

fig = plt.figure(figsize=(10, 8))
ax = fig.add_subplot(111, projection='3d')
ax.plot_surface(X, Y, Z, cmap='viridis', alpha=0.9)
ax.set_title('Möbius Strip')
ax.set_xlabel('X')
ax.set_ylabel('Y')
ax.set_zlabel('Z')
plt.savefig('mobius_strip.png', dpi=150)
plt.show()
