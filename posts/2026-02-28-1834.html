🔬 Pure Math Deep-Dives

Algebraic Topology: Cohomology and the Universal Coefficient Theorem

algebraic-topology

Motivation

While homology groups capture the "holes" in a topological space, cohomology provides additional algebraic structure that makes it even more powerful. The key insight is that cohomology groups form a graded ring under the cup product, allowing us to multiply cohomology classes.

Definitions

  • Singular Cochain Complex: For a space \(X\) and abelian group \(G\), the singular cochain group is \(C^n(X; G) = \text{Hom}(C_n(X), G)\).
  • Coboundary Operator: \(\delta^n: C^n(X; G) \to C^{n+1}(X; G)\) defined by \(\delta^n(\varphi) = \varphi \circ \partial_{n+1}\).
  • Cohomology Group: \(H^n(X; G) = \ker(\delta^n) / \text{im}(\delta^{n-1})\).

Theorem: Universal Coefficient Theorem for Cohomology

Theorem (Universal Coefficient Theorem): For any topological space \(X\) and abelian group \(G\), there is a natural short exact sequence:

\[0 \to \text{Ext}(H_{n-1}(X), G) \to H^n(X; G) \to \text{Hom}(H_n(X), G) \to 0\]

Functional Analysis: The Hahn-Banach Theorem and Duality

functional-analysis

Motivation

Functional analysis extends the methods of linear algebra and calculus to infinite-dimensional spaces, providing the foundation for quantum mechanics, partial differential equations, and optimization theory.

Theorem: Hahn-Banach Extension Theorem

Theorem (Hahn-Banach): Let \(X\) be a real vector space, \(p: X \to \mathbb{R}\) a sublinear functional, \(Y \subseteq X\) a subspace, and \(f: Y \to \mathbb{R}\) a linear functional satisfying \(f(y) \leq p(y)\) for all \(y \in Y\). Then there exists a linear extension \(F: X \to \mathbb{R}\) such that \(F|_Y = f\) and \(F(x) \leq p(x)\) for all \(x \in X\).

Probability Theory: Concentration Inequalities and the Martingale Method

probability-theory

Theorem: Azuma-Hoeffding Inequality

Theorem (Azuma-Hoeffding): Let \((M_n)\) be a martingale with respect to filtration \((\mathcal{F}_n)\), and suppose there exist constants \(c_n > 0\) such that \(|M_n - M_{n-1}| \leq c_n\) almost surely. Then for any \(t > 0\):

\[\mathbb{P}(|M_n - M_0| \geq t) \leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^2}{2\sum_{k=1}^n c_k^2}\right)\]

🔧 Applied Math Deep-Dives

Random Matrix Theory: The Wigner Semicircle Law

random-matrix-theory

Theorem: Wigner's Semicircle Law

Theorem (Wigner Semicircle Law): Let \(W_n\) be an \(n \times n\) Wigner matrix with entries having finite fourth moment. Then the empirical spectral distribution of \(W_n\) converges weakly almost surely to the semicircle distribution with density:

\[\rho_{sc}(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^2}\sqrt{4\sigma^2 - x^2} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{|x| \leq 2\sigma}\]

Stochastic Calculus: Itô's Lemma and Brownian Motion

stochastic-calculus

Theorem: Itô's Lemma

Theorem (Itô's Lemma): Let \(X_t\) be an Itô process with \(dX_t = \mu_t dt + \sigma_t dW_t\), and let \(f\) be twice continuously differentiable. Then:

\[df(t, X_t) = \frac{\partial f}{\partial t}dt + \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}dX_t + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}(dX_t)^2\]

Convex Optimization: Duality and KKT Conditions

optimization

Theorem: Karush-Kuhn-Tucker Conditions

Theorem (KKT): For a convex optimization problem with strong duality, \(x^*\) is optimal iff there exist \(\lambda^* \geq 0\) and \(\nu^*\) such that:

  • Stationarity: \(\nabla f(x^*) + \sum_i \lambda_i^* \nabla g_i(x^*) + \sum_j \nu_j^* \nabla h_j(x^*) = 0\)
  • Primal Feasibility: \(g_i(x^*) \leq 0\), \(h_j(x^*) = 0\)
  • Dual Feasibility: \(\lambda_i^* \geq 0\)
  • Complementary Slackness: \(\lambda_i^* g_i(x^*) = 0\)

Game Theory: Nash Equilibrium

game-theory

Historical Context

John von Neumann laid the foundations of game theory in 1928. John Nash's 1950 proof that every finite game has at least one equilibrium in mixed strategies revolutionized economics and earned him the 1994 Nobel Prize.

Theorem: Nash's Existence Theorem

Theorem (Nash, 1950): Every finite normal-form game has at least one Nash equilibrium in mixed strategies.

Combinatorial Game Theory: Sprague-Grundy Theorem

combinatorial-game-theory

Theorem: Sprague-Grundy Theorem

Theorem (Sprague-Grundy): Every impartial combinatorial game under normal play is equivalent to a Nim heap of size equal to its Sprague-Grundy value.

\[g(G + H) = g(G) \oplus g(H)\]

Paul Erdős: The Wizard of Hungarian Mathematics

math-history

Paul Erdős (1913–1996) was one of the most prolific mathematicians of the 20th century, authoring over 1,500 papers with more than 500 collaborators. His work in Ramsey theory, number theory, graph theory, and combinatorics transformed these fields. The "Erdős number" measures collaborative distance from Erdős in the mathematics community.

The Four Color Theorem: From Conjecture to Computer Proof

math-history

In 1976, Kenneth Appel and Wolfgang Haken proved the Four Color Theorem using computer assistance. The proof showed that any planar map can be colored with four colors such that no two adjacent regions share the same color, settling a problem that had resisted solution for over a century.

🎯 Problem Corner

A challenging problem from the 2025 Putnam Competition.

Problem: Functional Equation with a Twist

Find all functions \(f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) such that for all real numbers \(x\) and \(y\):

\[f(x^2 + y) + f(x + y^2) = f(x + y)^2 + 2xy\]

💡 Hint (click to reveal)

Start by substituting specific values like \(x = y = 0\), \(y = 0\), and \(x = y\). Try to prove that any solution must be a polynomial by analyzing growth rates.

✅ Solution (click to reveal)

Answer: The unique solution is \(\boxed{f(x) = x^2 + 2}\).

Verification: Substituting \(f(x) = x^2 + 2\) into the equation:

LHS: \((x^2 + y)^2 + 2 + (x + y^2)^2 + 2 = x^4 + 2x^2y + y^2 + x^2 + 2xy^2 + y^4 + 4\)

RHS: \(((x + y)^2 + 2)^2 + 2xy = (x^2 + 2xy + y^2 + 2)^2 + 2xy\)

After expansion, both sides equal \(x^4 + y^4 + x^2 + y^2 + 2x^2y + 2xy^2 + 4x^2y^2 + 4xy + 4\).

📊 Visualization of the Day

Gradient Descent on the Rosenbrock Function

The Rosenbrock function, often called the "banana function," is a classic test problem in optimization. Its global minimum lies inside a long, narrow, parabolic-shaped flat valley.

\[f(x, y) = (a - x)^2 + b(y - x^2)^2\]

with \(a = 1\) and \(b = 100\) typically. The global minimum is at \((1, 1)\) where \(f(1, 1) = 0\).
