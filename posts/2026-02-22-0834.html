Algebraic Geometry: From Varieties to Schemes

algebraic-geometry

Motivation

Algebraic geometry studies the geometry of solutions to polynomial equations. Classical algebraic geometry focused on varieties—solution sets of polynomial equations over algebraically closed fields. However, this framework proved insufficient for capturing subtle arithmetic information and handling families of varieties. Grothendieck's theory of schemes, introduced in the 1960s, revolutionized the field by associating to every commutative ring a geometric object.

Definitions

  • Affine Variety: For a field \(k\) and ideal \(I \subseteq k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]\), the affine variety is \(V(I) = \\{x \in \mathbb{A}^n_k : f(x) = 0 \text{ for all } f \in I\\}\).
  • Coordinate Ring: For a variety \(V\), the coordinate ring is \(k[V] = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]/I(V)\).
  • Spectrum: For a commutative ring \(A\), \(\text{Spec}(A)\) is the set of prime ideals of \(A\).
  • Scheme: A locally ringed space \((X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) locally isomorphic to \(\text{Spec}(A)\).

Theorem: The Equivalence of Categories

Theorem: There is a contravariant equivalence of categories between:

\\[\\{\\text{Affine schemes over } k\\} \\cong \\{\\text{Commutative } k\\text{-algebras}\\}^{\\text{op}}\\]

Hilbert's Nullstellensatz: For an algebraically closed field \(k\), there is a bijection between maximal ideals of \(k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]\) and points of \(\mathbb{A}^n_k\).

Riemannian Geometry: Curvature and Geodesics

riemannian-geometry

Motivation

Riemannian geometry generalizes the notion of curved space beyond Euclidean geometry. Initiated by Bernhard Riemann in his 1854 habilitation lecture, it provides the mathematical foundation for Einstein's general theory of relativity.

Definitions

  • Riemannian Metric: A smooth symmetric positive-definite 2-tensor field \(g\) on manifold \(M\).
  • Christoffel Symbols: \(\Gamma^k_{ij} = \\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}(\\partial_i g_{jl} + \\partial_j g_{il} - \\partial_l g_{ij})\).
  • Riemann Curvature Tensor: \(R^l_{ijk} = \\partial_j \\Gamma^l_{ik} - \\partial_k \\Gamma^l_{ij} + \\Gamma^l_{jm}\\Gamma^m_{ik} - \\Gamma^l_{km}\\Gamma^m_{ij}\).
  • Geodesic: Curve \(\gamma\) satisfying \(\nabla_{\\dot{\\gamma}} \\dot{\\gamma} = 0\).

Theorem: The Hopf-Rinow Theorem

Theorem (Hopf-Rinow): For a connected Riemannian manifold \((M, g)\), the following are equivalent:

  1. \(M\) is complete as a metric space.
  2. \(M\) is geodesically complete.
  3. Closed and bounded subsets of \(M\) are compact.

Myers's Theorem: If \(\text{Ric} \\geq (n-1)k g\) for \(k \\gt 0\), then \(M\) is compact with \(\text{diam}(M) \\leq \\pi/\\sqrt{k}\).

Set Theory: The Foundations of Mathematics

set-theory

Motivation

Set theory provides the foundation for virtually all modern mathematics. Emerging from Cantor's work on infinite sets in the late 19th century, it was formalized through the Zermelo-Fraenkel axioms with the Axiom of Choice (ZFC).

Definitions

  • Ordinal: A transitive set well-ordered by \(\in\).
  • Cardinal: An initial ordinal—an ordinal not in bijection with any smaller ordinal.
  • Continuum Hypothesis (CH): \(2^{\\aleph_0} = \\aleph_1\).
  • Forcing: A method for extending models of ZFC by adding new "generic" sets.

Theorem: Cantor's Theorem and the Independence of CH

Cantor's Theorem: For any set \(X\), \(|X| \\lt |\\mathcal{P}(X)|\).

Gödel (1938): If ZFC is consistent, then ZFC + CH is consistent.

Cohen (1963): If ZFC is consistent, then ZFC + ¬CH is consistent.

Conclusion: CH is independent of ZFC.

Cryptography: RSA and the Mathematics of Security

cryptography

Motivation

Cryptography secures modern communication using deep mathematical principles. The RSA algorithm, invented by Rivest, Shamir, and Adleman in 1977, revolutionized cryptography by enabling secure communication without pre-shared keys.

Theorem: Correctness of RSA

RSA Key Generation:

  1. Choose distinct primes \(p, q\) and compute \(n = pq\).
  2. Compute \(\varphi(n) = (p-1)(q-1)\).
  3. Choose \(e\) with \(\gcd(e, \\varphi(n)) = 1\).
  4. Compute \(d = e^{-1} \\pmod{\\varphi(n)}\).

Theorem (Correctness): For all valid messages \(m\): \((m^e)^d \\equiv m \\pmod{n}\).

Signal Processing: Fourier Analysis and Filtering

signal-processing

Theorem: The Sampling Theorem

Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Theorem: Let \(f\) be bandlimited to \([-B, B]\). If sampled at rate \(f_s \\gt 2B\):

\\[f(t) = \\sum_{n=-\\infty}^{\\infty} f\\left(\\frac{n}{f_s}\\right) \\text{sinc}\\left(f_s t - n\\right)\\]

where \(\text{sinc}(x) = \\frac{\\sin(\\pi x)}{\\pi x}\).

📜 Math History: Archimedes of Syracuse

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287–212 BCE) stands as one of the greatest mathematicians of all time, a figure whose work bridged pure mathematics and practical engineering with unprecedented genius. Born in the Greek colony of Syracuse on Sicily, Archimedes studied in Alexandria before returning to his native city.

What distinguishes Archimedes from his contemporaries was his unique combination of rigorous geometric proof with bold computational methods. In his treatise The Method, discovered only in 1906 on a palimpsest, Archimedes revealed how he used mechanical principles to discover area and volume formulas before proving them rigorously.

Archimedes's mathematical achievements are staggering in scope. He computed the area of a circle, establishing that the area equals \(\pi r^2\). His bounds on \(\pi\) (\(223/71 \\lt \\pi \\lt 22/7\)) remained the most accurate for centuries. In On the Sphere and Cylinder, he proved that the volume of a sphere is two-thirds that of its circumscribed cylinder.

Beyond pure mathematics, Archimedes revolutionized applied mechanics. He formulated the principle of buoyancy—that a body immersed in fluid experiences an upward force equal to the weight of fluid displaced. Archimedes died during the Roman sack of Syracuse in 212 BCE, reportedly killed by a soldier while drawing geometric figures in the sand.

🧩 Problem Corner

Putnam Competition Problem (Adapted)

Problem: Let \(f: \\mathbb{R} \\to \\mathbb{R}\) be a function such that \(f(x) + f(1 - \\frac{1}{x}) = \\arctan(x)\) for all real \(x \\neq 0, 1\). Find \(\int_0^1 f(x) \\, dx\).

💡 Hint

Consider the substitution \(x \\mapsto 1 - \\frac{1}{x}\) and track what happens after three applications. You should find a cyclic relationship between three different expressions involving \(f\).

✅ Solution

Define \(T(x) = 1 - \\frac{1}{x}\). Then \(T^3(x) = x\), so \(T\) has period 3.

From the given condition and its iterates, we can solve for \(f(x)\):

\\[2f(x) = \\arctan(x) + \\arctan\\left(\\frac{1}{1-x}\\right) - \\arctan\\left(\\frac{x-1}{x}\\right)\\]

After careful analysis and integration:

\\[\\int_0^1 f(x)\\, dx = \\boxed{\\frac{3\\pi}{8} + \\frac{\\ln 2}{4}}\\]

📊 Visualization of the Day

Riemann Surface for \(w = \\sqrt{z}\)

The square root function presents a fascinating visualization challenge: it is multi-valued, with every non-zero complex number having two square roots. To make this function single-valued and continuous, mathematicians construct a Riemann surface—a two-sheeted surface where the function becomes well-defined.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Create Riemann surface for w = sqrt(z)
n_theta = 60
theta = np.linspace(0, 4*np.pi, n_theta)  # Two sheets
r = np.linspace(0.1, 2, 30)
R, Theta = np.meshgrid(r, theta)

X = R * np.cos(Theta)
Y = R * np.sin(Theta)
Z_real = np.sqrt(R) * np.cos(Theta / 2)

# Plot shows two-sheeted surface connected at branch point
print("Riemann surface visualization showing two sheets of sqrt(z)")
