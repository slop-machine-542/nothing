🔬 Pure Math Deep-Dives

1. Homological Algebra: The Five Lemma

The Five Lemma is a fundamental result in homological algebra that relates the exactness of two short exact sequences connected by homomorphisms.

Five Lemma: Consider a commutative diagram with exact rows: \[\begin{array}{ccccccccc} A & \xrightarrow{f} & B & \xrightarrow{g} & C & \xrightarrow{h} & D & \xrightarrow{k} & E \\ \downarrow{\alpha} & & \downarrow{\beta} & & \downarrow{\gamma} & & \downarrow{\delta} & & \downarrow{\epsilon} \\ A' & \xrightarrow{f'} & B' & \xrightarrow{g'} & C' & \xrightarrow{h'} & D' & \xrightarrow{k'} & E' \end{array}\] If \(\alpha, \beta, \delta, \epsilon\) are isomorphisms, then \(\gamma\) is also an isomorphism.

2. Category Theory: Limits and Colimits

Limits and colimits are universal constructions in category theory that generalize products, coproducts, pullbacks, pushouts, and more.

Definition of Limit: Let \(F: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{C}\) be a diagram. A limit of \(F\) is a cone \((L, \{\lambda_j\})\) such that for any other cone \((N, \{\psi_j\})\), there exists a unique morphism \(u: N \to L\) making all triangles commute.

3. Functional Analysis: The Open Mapping Theorem

The Open Mapping Theorem states that a surjective continuous linear operator between Banach spaces is an open map.

Open Mapping Theorem: Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be Banach spaces and \(T: X \to Y\) be a surjective continuous linear operator. Then \(T\) is an open map: if \(U\) is open in \(X\), then \(T(U)\) is open in \(Y\). 
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import lstsq

def open_mapping_principle():
    """
    Demonstrate the open mapping principle numerically.
    For a surjective linear map T: R^n -> R^m, the image of 
    an open ball contains an open ball around 0.
    """
    # Define surjective linear map T: R^3 -> R^2
    T_matrix = np.array([[1, 0, 1],
                         [0, 1, 1]])
    
    # Check surjectivity: rank should equal m = 2
    rank = np.linalg.matrix_rank(T_matrix)
    print(f"Rank of T: {rank}")
    print(f"T is surjective: {rank == 2}")
    
    # Image of unit ball under T
    # For unit vector x, ||Tx|| can be as small as smallest singular value
    U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(T_matrix)
    print(f"Singular values: {S}")
    print(f"Smallest singular value: {S[-1]:.6f}")
    print(f"Image of unit ball contains ball of radius: {S[-1]:.6f}")

open_mapping_principle()

🧮 Applied Math Deep-Dives

1. Numerical Analysis: LU Decomposition

LU decomposition factors a matrix as the product of a lower triangular matrix and an upper triangular matrix, useful for solving systems of linear equations.

LU Decomposition: For any invertible matrix \(A\), there exists a permutation matrix \(P\), a lower triangular matrix \(L\) with ones on the diagonal, and an upper triangular matrix \(U\) such that \(PA = LU\). 
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import lu

def lu_decomposition_demo():
    """
    Demonstrate LU decomposition for solving linear systems.
    """
    # Create a matrix
    A = np.array([[4, 3],
                  [6, 3]], dtype=float)
    b = np.array([10, 12], dtype=float)
    
    # LU decomposition with partial pivoting
    P, L, U = lu(A)
    
    print("Original matrix A:")
    print(A)
    print("\nPermutation matrix P:")
    print(P)
    print("\nLower triangular L:")
    print(L)
    print("\nUpper triangular U:")
    print(U)
    
    # Verify: PA = LU
    print("\nVerification: PA = LU?")
    print(np.allclose(P @ A, L @ U))
    
    # Solve using forward/backward substitution
    # PAx = Pb => LUx = Pb
    # Let y = Ux, then Ly = Pb
    Pb = P @ b
    y = np.linalg.solve(L, Pb)
    x = np.linalg.solve(U, y)
    
    print(f"\nSolution: x = {x}")
    print(f"Verification: Ax = {A @ x}")

lu_decomposition_demo()

2. Optimization: Convex Functions

Convex functions play a central role in optimization because any local minimum is also a global minimum.

Definition of Convexity: A function \(f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}\) is convex if for all \(x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n\) and \(\lambda \in [0,1]\): \[f(\lambda x + (1-\lambda)y) \leq \lambda f(x) + (1-\lambda)f(y)\]

3. Signal Processing: The Discrete Fourier Transform

The DFT transforms a sequence of complex numbers into another sequence of complex numbers, representing the signal in the frequency domain.

Discrete Fourier Transform: For a sequence \(x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_{N-1}\), the DFT is: \[X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n e^{-2\pi i kn/N}, \quad k = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1\]

🌟 Special Topics

1. The Continuum Hypothesis

The Continuum Hypothesis states that there is no set whose cardinality is strictly between that of the integers and the real numbers.

Continuum Hypothesis (CH): \(2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1\), i.e., there is no cardinal number strictly between \(\aleph_0\) and \(2^{\aleph_0}\).

2. The Hodge Conjecture

The Hodge conjecture is a major unsolved problem in algebraic geometry that relates the algebraic topology of a non-singular complex algebraic variety to its subvarieties.

3. The Yang-Mills Existence and Mass Gap

One of the Millennium Prize Problems, this concerns the quantum version of Maxwell's equations and the existence of a "mass gap" in the spectrum of quantum Yang-Mills theory.
