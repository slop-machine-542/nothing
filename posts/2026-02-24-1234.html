🔬 Pure Math Deep-Dives

1. Spectral Graph Theory: Algebra Meets Combinatorics

Tags: spectral-graph-theory linear-algebra combinatorics

Motivation

Spectral graph theory studies the relationship between the structural properties of a graph and the eigenvalues of matrices associated with it. This field emerged in the mid-20th century, connecting algebraic methods to combinatorial problems.

Definitions

Let \(G = (V, E)\) be an undirected graph with \(n\) vertices. The adjacency matrix \(A\) is defined by: \(A_{ij} = 1\) if \((i,j) \in E\), 0 otherwise.

The Laplacian matrix is \(L = D - A\) where \(D\) is the degree matrix.

The spectral gap is \(\lambda_2\), the second smallest eigenvalue of \(L\).

Theorem (Cheeger Inequality for Graphs)

Let \(G\) be a connected graph with normalized Laplacian eigenvalues \(0 = \lambda_1 \leq \lambda_2 \leq \cdots \leq \lambda_n\). Let \(\phi(G)\) be the conductance. Then: \(\frac{\phi(G)^2}{2} \leq \lambda_2 \leq 2\phi(G)\)

Proof Sketch

For the upper bound, use the test function that is constant on each side of the cut. For the lower bound, use the co-area formula and the variational characterization of eigenvalues.

2. Morse Theory: Topology Through Critical Points

Tags: morse-theory differential-topology algebraic-topology

Motivation

Morse theory, developed by Marston Morse in the 1920s-30s, provides a profound connection between the critical points of smooth functions on a manifold and the topology of that manifold.

Definitions

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold and \(f: M \to \mathbb{R}\) a smooth function. A point \(p \in M\) is critical if \(df_p = 0\).

The Hessian at a critical point is the symmetric bilinear form \(H_f(X, Y) = X(Yf)(p)\).

The index of a non-degenerate critical point is the number of negative eigenvalues of the Hessian.

Theorem (Morse Lemma)

Let \(f: M \to \mathbb{R}\) be smooth and \(p\) a non-degenerate critical point of index \(k\). Then there exist local coordinates such that: \(f(x) = f(p) - x_1^2 - \cdots - x_k^2 + x_{k+1}^2 + \cdots + x_n^2\)

3. Ramsey Theory: Order in Chaos

Tags: ramsey-theory combinatorics graph-theory

Motivation

Ramsey theory, named after Frank Ramsey (1930), is the study of conditions under which order must appear. The central philosophy is that complete disorder is impossible.

Definitions

The Ramsey number \(R(s, t)\) is the smallest \(n\) such that every red-blue coloring of \(K_n\) contains a red \(K_s\) or blue \(K_t\).

Theorem (Ramsey's Theorem)

For all \(s, t \geq 2\), the Ramsey number \(R(s, t)\) is finite. Moreover: \(R(s, t) \leq R(s-1, t) + R(s, t-1)\)

1. Epidemiological Modeling: Mathematics of Disease Spread

Tags: epidemiological-modeling differential-equations mathematical-biology

The SIR Model

The classic SIR model divides a population into Susceptible \(S(t)\), Infected \(I(t)\), and Recovered \(R(t)\).

Theorem (Threshold Theorem)

For the SIR model, an epidemic occurs iff \(\mathcal{R}_0 > 1\). The final epidemic size satisfies: \(R_\infty = 1 - e^{-\mathcal{R}_0 R_\infty}\)

2. Portfolio Optimization: The Mathematics of Investment

Tags: portfolio-optimization mathematical-finance optimization

Mean-Variance Optimization

Let \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\) be expected returns and \(\Sigma\) the covariance matrix. The portfolio variance is \(\sigma_p^2 = \mathbf{w}^T \Sigma \mathbf{w}\).

Theorem (Two-Fund Theorem)

Any portfolio on the efficient frontier can be constructed as a linear combination of two distinct efficient portfolios.

📜 Math History: Évariste Galois (1811–1832)

In the early hours of May 30, 1832, a young Frenchman named Évariste Galois was shot in a duel. He died the next day at age 20, leaving behind mathematical papers that would revolutionize algebra.

Galois's central achievement was developing a theory that determines exactly which polynomial equations are solvable by radicals. He associated to each equation a group—the Galois group—and showed that the structure of this group determines solvability.

The night before the duel, Galois stayed up writing, annotating his papers with desperate marginal notes: "I have no time, I have no time." His work was only published in 1846 by Liouville, and has since become fundamental to modern algebra.

🧩 Problem Corner

Competition Problem: Putnam 2023 B5

Problem: Let \(n\) be a positive integer. A sequence \((a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n)\) of integers is called good if: (1) \(0 \leq a_k \leq k\) for each \(k\), and (2) \(a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n\) is even. Determine the number of good sequences in terms of \(n\).

💡 Hint

Consider the generating function approach. For each position \(k\), the generating function is \(1 + x + x^2 + \cdots + x^k\). Use the trick of evaluating at \(x = 1\) and \(x = -1\).

📖 Solution

Solution: Let \(f(x) = \prod_{k=1}^{n} (1 + x + \cdots + x^k)\). The total number of sequences is \(f(1) = (n+1)!\). The difference between even and odd sums is \(f(-1) = 0\) (for \(n \geq 1\)). Thus the answer is \(\boxed{\frac{(n+1)!}{2}}\).

🎨 Visualization of the Day: The Mandelbrot Set

Today's visualization explores the Mandelbrot set, one of the most famous fractals in mathematics. The set consists of complex numbers \(c\) for which the iteration \(z_{n+1} = z_n^2 + c\) starting from \(z_0 = 0\) remains bounded.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def mandelbrot(c, max_iter=100):
    z = 0
    for n in range(max_iter):
        if abs(z) > 2:
            return n
        z = z*z + c
    return max_iter

# Generate the set
x = np.linspace(-2.5, 1.5, 1000)
y = np.linspace(-1.5, 1.5, 1000)
X, Y = np.meshgrid(x, y)
C = X + 1j*Y
M = np.vectorize(mandelbrot)(C)

plt.imshow(M, extent=[-2.5, 1.5, -1.5, 1.5], cmap='hot')
plt.title('Mandelbrot Set')
plt.show()
