🧮 Pure Mathematics Deep-Dives

Galois Theory: The Mathematics of Symmetry in Equations

Motivation: Why can some polynomial equations be solved by radicals while others cannot? This question plagued mathematicians for centuries until Évariste Galois, at age 20, developed a theory that connects field extensions to group theory. Galois theory provides a complete answer to the solvability problem and reveals deep connections between algebra and symmetry.

Definitions

  • Field Extension: A pair of fields \(F \subseteq E\) where \(E\) is a vector space over \(F\). The degree \([E:F]\) is the dimension of this vector space.
  • Splitting Field: For a polynomial \(f \in F[x]\), the smallest extension \(E/F\) such that \(f\) splits completely into linear factors over \(E\).
  • Galois Group: \(\text{Gal}(E/F) = \{\sigma \in \text{Aut}(E) : \sigma|_F = \text{id}_F\}\), the group of field automorphisms fixing \(F\) pointwise.
  • Separable Extension: An algebraic extension where every element has a minimal polynomial with distinct roots (no repeated roots).
  • Normal Extension: An algebraic extension where every irreducible polynomial with a root in the extension splits completely.

Theorem: The Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory

Theorem: Let \(E/F\) be a finite Galois extension with Galois group \(G = \text{Gal}(E/F)\). There is a one-to-one correspondence between: \[\{\text{Intermediate fields } K : F \subseteq K \subseteq E\} \longleftrightarrow \{\text{Subgroups } H \leq G\}\] given by \(K \mapsto \text{Gal}(E/K)\) and \(H \mapsto E^H\) (the fixed field of \(H\)).

Moreover:

  • The correspondence is inclusion-reversing
  • \([E:K] = |\text{Gal}(E/K)|\) and \([K:F] = [G : \text{Gal}(E/K)]\)
  • \(K/F\) is Galois if and only if \(\text{Gal}(E/K) \trianglelefteq G\)
  • When \(K/F\) is Galois, \(\text{Gal}(K/F) \cong G/\text{Gal}(E/K)\)
