🔗 Math Links

MathOverflow

A question and answer site for professional mathematicians. Unlike general Q&A platforms, MathOverflow is specifically designed for research-level mathematics, with a community of active researchers, professors, and advanced students. The site maintains high standards for content quality, and discussions often involve cutting-edge developments in pure and applied mathematics. It is an invaluable resource for understanding current research trends and finding expert perspectives on complex mathematical questions.

Khan Academy Mathematics

A comprehensive free educational platform offering interactive lessons, exercises, and videos covering mathematics from basic arithmetic through multivariable calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations. The adaptive learning system personalizes the educational experience, while detailed progress tracking helps learners identify areas for improvement. Khan Academy's mastery-based approach ensures deep understanding before progression, making it an excellent resource for both supplementing formal education and self-study.

Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society

The Bulletin publishes expository articles on contemporary mathematical research, written in a way that gives insight to mathematicians who may not be experts in a particular area. The articles cover significant developments in pure and applied mathematics, including surveys of important results, historical perspectives, and discussions of open problems. The Bulletin is freely available online and serves as an excellent entry point for understanding modern mathematical research across diverse fields.

📚 Free Math Book

"p-adic Numbers: An Introduction"

Author: Fernando Q. Gouvêa

Level: Undergraduate/Graduate

Availability: Springer (Open Access)

Summary

This engaging introduction to p-adic numbers takes readers from elementary number theory to the frontiers of modern research. Gouvêa's expository style makes the counterintuitive world of p-adic analysis accessible while maintaining mathematical rigor.

The book begins with Ostrowski's theorem classifying absolute values on the rational numbers, then constructs the p-adic numbers as completions of \\(\\mathbb{Q}\\) under the p-adic absolute value. It explores the topology of p-adic spaces—where every point is the center of a ball and all triangles are isosceles—and develops the theory of p-adic power series, including Strassmann's theorem on zeros. Later chapters connect p-adic analysis to algebraic number theory, Diophantine equations, and even physics. The text includes numerous exercises and historical notes, making it ideal for self-study or as a course textbook.
