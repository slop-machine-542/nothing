Spectral Theory: The Harmonic Analysis of Operators

spectral-theory

Motivation

Spectral theory extends the concept of eigenvalues from finite-dimensional linear algebra to infinite-dimensional spaces. Just as a symmetric matrix can be diagonalized, self-adjoint operators on Hilbert spaces admit a spectral decomposition. This theory, pioneered by Hilbert and developed by von Neumann, provides the mathematical foundation for quantum mechanics.

Definitions

  • Resolvent Set: For a linear operator \(T\), \(\rho(T) = \{\lambda \in \mathbb{C} : (T - \lambda I)^{-1} \text{ exists and is bounded}\}\).
  • Spectrum: \(\sigma(T) = \mathbb{C} \setminus \rho(T)\).
  • Point Spectrum: Eigenvalues \(\lambda\) where \(T - \lambda I\) is not injective.
  • Spectral Radius: \(r(T) = \sup\{|\lambda| : \lambda \in \sigma(T)\}\).

Theorem: The Spectral Theorem

Theorem: Let \(T\) be a bounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space \(H\). Then there exists a unique spectral measure \(E\) such that:

\[T = \int_{\sigma(T)} \lambda \, dE(\lambda)\]

Proof Sketch

The proof proceeds by establishing a functional calculus for polynomials, extending via Stone-Weierstrass to continuous functions, then constructing the spectral measure via the Riesz representation theorem.

Notation Guide

  • \(\sigma(T)\) — spectrum of operator
  • \(\rho(T)\) — resolvent set
  • \(R_\lambda(T) = (T - \lambda I)^{-1}\) — resolvent
  • \(r(T)\) — spectral radius

Ergodic Theory: Long-Term Behavior

ergodic-theory

Motivation

Ergodic theory studies dynamical systems from a statistical perspective, asking when time averages equal space averages. Originating in Boltzmann's statistical mechanics, it has grown into a rich field connecting dynamical systems, probability, number theory, and harmonic analysis.

Definitions

  • Measure-Preserving System: \((X, \mathcal{B}, \mu, T)\) where \(\mu(T^{-1}(A)) = \mu(A)\).
  • Ergodicity: Every invariant set has measure 0 or 1.
  • Time Average: \(\bar{f}(x) = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} f(T^n x)\).

Theorem: Birkhoff's Ergodic Theorem

Theorem: For a measure-preserving system and \(f \in L^1\), the time average exists a.e. and equals the space average for ergodic systems:

\[\bar{f}(x) = \int_X f \, d\mu\]

Symplectic Geometry: Phase Space

symplectic-geometry

Motivation

Symplectic geometry is the mathematical framework for classical mechanics, providing the natural setting for Hamilton's equations. Unlike Riemannian geometry, symplectic geometry preserves area-like structures in phase space.

Definitions

  • Symplectic Form: A closed, non-degenerate 2-form \(\omega\).
  • Hamiltonian Vector Field: \(X_H\) defined by \(\omega(X_H, \cdot) = dH\).
  • Poisson Bracket: \(\{f, g\} = \omega(X_f, X_g)\).

Theorem: Darboux's Theorem

Theorem: Near any point, there exist coordinates where \(\omega = \sum dq_i \wedge dp_i\).

Control Theory: The Mathematics of Feedback

control-theory

Real-World Motivation

Control theory provides the mathematical foundation for systems that regulate themselves — from thermostats to spacecraft. The core idea is feedback: measuring the difference between desired and actual behavior, then adjusting inputs.

Definitions

  • State-Space: \(\dot{x} = Ax + Bu\), \(y = Cx + Du\).
  • Controllability: Any state reachable from any other state.
  • Observability: Initial state determined from output history.

Theorem: Pole Placement

Theorem: If \((A, B)\) is controllable, there exists feedback \(K\) placing eigenvalues of \(A - BK\) at desired locations.

Game Theory: Strategic Interaction

Game theory studies strategic interactions where outcomes depend on all participants' choices. From auctions to evolution, it analyzes rational agents making interdependent decisions.

  • Dominant Strategy: Optimal regardless of others' actions.

📜 Math History

Leonhard Euler: The Master of Us All

Leonhard Euler (1707–1783) stands as the most prolific mathematician in history, with over 800 books and papers published during his lifetime. Born in Basel, Switzerland, Euler's genius was recognized early by Johann Bernoulli.

Euler's contributions are staggering. In analysis, he introduced the concept of a function and established the notation \(f(x)\). He connected exponential and trigonometric functions through his famous formula \(e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta\).

What makes Euler's achievement even more remarkable is that much of his later work was done while blind. After losing his right eye in 1738 and becoming completely blind in 1766, Euler's productivity actually increased.

Euler introduced \(\pi\) for the circle constant, \(e\) for natural logarithms, \(i\) for \(\sqrt{-1}\), and \(\Sigma\) for summation. As Laplace advised: "Read Euler, read Euler. He is the master of us all."

🎯 Problem Corner

Competition Problem: Good Sequences

Let \(n\) be a positive integer. A sequence \(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n\) is called good if \(1 \leq a_k \leq k\) and if \(a_k > 1\) then \(a_k - 1\) appears earlier. Let \(f(n)\) be the number of good sequences. Find a closed-form.

Hint

Try small cases: \(f(1) = 1\), \(f(2) = 2\), \(f(3) = 5\). These are the Catalan numbers!

Solution

We claim \(f(n) = C_n = \frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}\). Establish a bijection to Dyck paths. Given a good sequence, construct a Dyck path where \(a_k\) determines the height after the \(k\)-th up-step. The constraints ensure the path never goes below the x-axis.

The Hopf Fibration: S³ → S²

The Hopf fibration is one of the most beautiful structures in mathematics — a continuous mapping from the 3-sphere to the 2-sphere where each point has a circle as its preimage. These fibers are all linked with each other.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def hopf_map(theta, phi, psi):
    # Point on S²
    x_s2 = np.sin(theta) * np.cos(phi)
    y_s2 = np.sin(theta) * np.sin(phi)  
    z_s2 = np.cos(theta)
    return (x_s2, y_s2, z_s2)

# The Hopf fibration shows π₃(S²) = Z
print("Hopf fibration: S³ → S²")
print("Each fiber is a great circle in S³")
print("Any two distinct fibers are linked")
