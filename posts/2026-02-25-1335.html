1. Lie Algebras: The Infinitesimal Structure of Symmetry

Tags: lie-algebras representation-theory

Motivation

Lie algebras are the linearized versions of Lie groups, capturing the infinitesimal structure of continuous symmetries. They were introduced by Sophus Lie in the 1880s to study transformation groups and have become fundamental tools in physics, geometry, and representation theory.

Definitions

  • Lie Algebra: A vector space \( \mathfrak{g} \) over a field \( F \) equipped with a bilinear operation \( [\cdot, \cdot]: \mathfrak{g} \times \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g} \) called the Lie bracket, satisfying:
    • Antisymmetry: \( [X, Y] = -[Y, X] \)
    • Jacobi identity: \( [X, [Y, Z]] + [Y, [Z, X]] + [Z, [X, Y]] = 0 \)
  • Killing Form: The symmetric bilinear form \( \kappa(X, Y) = \text{tr}(\text{ad}_X \circ \text{ad}_Y) \).

Theorem: Cartan's Criterion

Theorem: A finite-dimensional Lie algebra \( \mathfrak{g} \) over a field of characteristic zero is semisimple if and only if its Killing form is nondegenerate.

        self.basis = basis
        self.dim = len(basis)
        self.bracket_table = bracket_table

        result = np.zeros(self.dim)
